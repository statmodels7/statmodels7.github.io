--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHY: THE TOOLKIT'S OWN ARGUMENT, TURNED ON ITSELF
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The positioning of statmodels7 is that modelling packages rewrite the same
components privately, and that writing them once as reusable objects is the
whole point. The census of 2026-08-05 shows the toolkit doing internally the
exact thing it condemns:

  - set_partitions() exists TWICE, in distributions7/R/expected_derivatives.R
    and in parameters7/R/jet.R -- the second written without knowing about
    the first, which is precisely how private helpers breed.
  - finite-difference machinery exists THREE times, independently:
    linkfunctions7 (fd_step, stencil_deriv, fallback_deriv, analytic_order),
    basis7 (fd_weights, fd_offsets -- general Fornberg-style stencil weights,
    the most general of the three), and distributions7 (fd_steps,
    numerical_gradient, numerical_hessian, plus fd5_first..fd5_fourth sitting
    inside skewt_distrib.R).
  - basis7 carries a Gauss-Legendre rule (gauss_legendre, quad_rule) while
    distributions7 does every integral through stats::integrate, one theta at
    a time, unaware the fixed-node rule exists one package over.
  - jets -- 591 lines, 21 functions, automatic differentiation to fourth
    order with Ops/Math dispatch -- live in parameters7, though nothing about
    them concerns constrained parameters; they landed there because their
    first consumer (the Levinson-Durbin recursion) did.
  - scattered one-per-family: owen_t and mills_ratio (skewnormal1), the
    Bessel ratio A and its four derivatives (vonmises1/2), numerical_series,
    the GRoU sampler and its anchors, compositions, leibniz_gram,
    tuple_indices.

A guard that lives in one package out of three is not a habit; a stencil that
lives in three packages in three versions is not a stencil library.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE FLAGSHIP: QUADRATURE AND SERIES VECTORIZED OVER THETA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The package is not justified by tidying alone. The new capability is an
integrator whose cost does not multiply by the number of parameter values.

Measured motivation (2026-08-05, this machine): variance() of the von Mises
-- a moment still on the quadrature fallback -- costs 25 ms per theta and
scales linearly: 12.7 s for 500 thetas. The truncated gaussian's expected
Hessian costs 0.5 s at 100 thetas. In a regression model theta varies by
observation, so these are per-iteration costs proportional to n. The
integrand evaluations themselves are microseconds; the overhead is one
stats::integrate call per theta.

quad_vec(f, lower, upper, theta, rule = gauss_kronrod(15), tol, max_depth):

  - Fixed nodes u_k with weights on [-1, 1], mapped per theta:
    X[i, k] = m_i + h_i * u_k. ONE call f(X, theta) evaluates everything --
    this works because the toolkit's densities are already vectorized in
    both the response and the parameters, which is the property the whole
    design leans on.
  - Integral by rowSums(F * w) * h; error estimate from the embedded
    lower-order rule of the Gauss-Kronrod pair.
  - Adaptivity BATCHED BY ROW: the rows whose error exceeds tol have their
    panels split, and all new panels across all failing rows are gathered
    into the next single matrix call. A pathological theta refines its own
    panels without serializing the others.
  - Infinite domains through the quantile-anchored splits expectation()
    already uses (the quantile functions are vectorized), and the rational
    edge maps of book section 3.4 where a density diverges at an endpoint.
  - Refusal over plausibility: a row that has not converged at max_depth is
    NA with a message naming WHICH theta, in keeping with "an error is worth
    more than a plausible number" (the nine-knot Gamma lesson).

series_vec(term, theta, tol, max_terms):

  - term(k, theta) evaluated in blocks, an outer matrix per block; per-row
    accumulation with a convergence mask so finished rows stop costing;
    a tail guard (ratio or alternating) so a slowly-thinning series is not
    declared converged by a small last term; rows not converged at max_terms
    are NA with a message. Consumers: the NB1 series, discrete expectations,
    disc_cum_table.

Consumers to rewire in distributions7, in order of pain relieved:
expectation()/moment(), truncated()'s Z and E-terms, the "integrate" approx
of expected derivatives, the cdf quadrature fallback, numerical_series.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHAT MOVES, BY PHASE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 1 -- the repo and the algebra layer.
  Repository statmodels7/numericals7, workflows, Pages ENABLED immediately
  after creation (gh api -X POST .../pages), test-docs.R from the shared
  source, NEWS.md from the first commit, logo tracked. Contents: the whole
  jet vocabulary with Ops/Math/print (from parameters7), set_partitions (ONE
  copy), the tuple/position enumeration jet_layout needs, compositions.
  parameters7 keeps param_tuple_indices as API but implements it on the
  numericals7 enumeration, so the two cannot disagree.

Phase 2 -- one stencil library.
  basis7's fd_weights/fd_offsets are the general construction (weights for
  any order at any offsets) and become the core; fd5_* become instances;
  fd_step/fd_steps unify the step-size rules. The policy stays where it is
  enforced: "one stencil of order k-m on the highest analytic order, never a
  chain of first differences" is linkfunctions7's and distributions7's
  business; numericals7 supplies the stencil, not the policy.

Phase 3 -- quad_vec and series_vec, as in section 1. Verified against
  stats::integrate at scalar theta (an independent implementation), against
  closed forms (gaussian moments, gamma moments), and with paired
  injections: a rule with one weight wrong by 5% must fail.

Phase 4 -- special functions: owen_t, mills_ratio (log scale), the Bessel
  ratio A(kappa) with its four derivatives and its inverse. Each keeps the
  overflow discipline recorded on it (expon.scaled, log-scale Mills).

Phase 5 -- candidates to decide later, not blockers: the GRoU sampler and
  its anchors (movable -- it takes only a log-density and bounds -- but
  entangled with distrib conventions on atoms and bounds), disc_cum_table.

Does NOT move: deriv_names/hess_names and every distribution's bookkeeping,
the validators (check_*), anything dispatching on distrib/basis/parameter
classes, family-specific moment formulas.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE DEPENDENCY GRAPH, AND WHAT IT COSTS AT CRAN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

numericals7 becomes the root of the toolkit:

  numericals7  ->  linkfunctions7 (stencils), parameters7 (jets),
                   basis7 (stencils, quadrature), distributions7 (all of it)
  optimizers7: its finite differences are compiled and self-contained;
  verify during phase 2 and do NOT force a dependency it does not need.

Consequences, stated rather than discovered later:
  - The CRAN order gains a step: numericals7 first, then linkfunctions7 and
    optimizers7 (still parallel with each other), then distributions7.
    Task #66 changes accordingly.
  - parameters7 loses jet.R: version 0.5.0, autoregressive() rewires to
    numericals7. Clean cut, no aliases -- nothing is on CRAN and the house
    rule is that a clean cut beats aliases.
  - Version discipline is not optional: TODAY'S lesson is that adding
    exports without bumping the version left a dependent's CI installing a
    stale cache keyed on the unchanged version, red on coverage only. Every
    phase that adds exports bumps the minor version, and every consumer
    states the version it needs. Push the DEPENDENCY before the consumer.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. ORDER OF WORK, AND WHERE THE BOOK FITS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phases 1-3, then the consumer migration package by package -- during which
the old implementations serve as the independent reference for the new ones
(two implementations of one object, the comparison that needs no tolerance),
and are deleted only after the comparison passes. Then ONE propagation:
the pending #73 (fifteen new families, numbered names, closed-form moments,
reparametrize) plus numericals7's arrival, so the book is re-rendered once;
the book eventually takes a numericals7 chapter and sections 2.x/3.4 change
attribution when the fallbacks' machinery moves. The American-English sweep
(#74) runs after, over one consistent state.
