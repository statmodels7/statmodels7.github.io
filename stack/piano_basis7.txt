--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT IT IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

basis7 is a *base* package of the toolkit, in the same sense as linkfunctions7:
it knows nothing about models, nothing about penalties, nothing about data. It
provides basis expansions -- B-splines, Fourier, orthogonal polynomials -- as S7
objects that can be evaluated, differentiated and integrated, and that know
their own inner products.

    depends on:      S7, splines2 (see 6a)
    depended on by:  penalties7   (consumes basis_gram for integral penalties)
                     modelterms7  (a term = basis + penalty + data mapping)
                     statmodels7  (through the above)

The positioning argument is the standard one, and it holds here with unusual
force. Every R package that fits smooth terms carries its own basis code:
mgcv's smooth constructors, gamlss's pb(), fda's create.*.basis, each private,
each exposing exactly what its author needed. A cubic B-spline is a fixed
mathematical object. It should be written once, correctly, with everything a
modelling routine could want already computed -- the design matrix, every
derivative, the antiderivative, and the Gram matrices of its derivatives, which
is what a smoothness penalty integrates. Nobody else's basis objects carry the
inner products; this one does, exactly.

What basis7 is NOT:

  - It is not the penalty layer. The Gram matrix G_d = int B^(d) B^(d)' dx is
    basis mathematics (an inner product) and lives here; a *penalty* -- which
    matrix a term is shrunk by, the discrete difference penalties D'D of
    Eilers-Marx that need no integral at all, the smoothing-parameter machinery
    -- is model semantics and lives in penalties7. penalties7 will consume
    basis_gram for O'Sullivan-type integral penalties. This settles the
    prototype's ldo()/gpf() question: the mathematics stays, renamed to what it
    is (a weighted Gram matrix), and nothing here is called a penalty.

  - It is not the identifiability layer. Bases here are COMPLETE (a B-spline
    basis carries all K functions and sums to one). Dropping a column, or
    imposing a sum-to-zero constraint, is a per-term model decision and belongs
    to modelterms7, where mgcv also puts it. Consequence: no `intercept`
    argument anywhere (see 6b).

  - Multivariate bases ARE in scope, and they live HERE (Giovanni,
    2026-08-03, explicit -- reversing this plan's first draft, which parked
    them behind modelterms7). The construction of a D-variate basis is basis
    mathematics: the classic tensor product, its partial derivatives, its
    separable Gram matrices, and the contracted evaluation that never
    materializes the Kronecker product. What stays upstream is only what
    involves model PARAMETERS: the factorized-coefficient predictor of
    Ruegamer (2024) keeps its latent factors in modelterms7, its
    df-calibrated smoothing in penalties7, and its optimiser in optimizers7
    (which already ships adam()). See section 3b.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. REVIEW OF THE PROTOTYPE -- what carries over, what gets fixed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three ideas carry over unchanged in substance:

  (a) The Fourier phase-shift evaluator. With z = 2*pi*(x - lower)/omega, the
      d-th derivative of every column is sin(j z + d*pi/2) * (2*pi*j/omega)^d,
      one formula for all orders, exact, and valid for d = -1 as the
      antiderivative. This is the closed form the package ships.

  (b) The orthonormalisation WRAPPER. An object that wraps a parent basis and
      transforms evaluation, derivatives and integrals consistently is the
      transformation() pattern from distributions7, and it is the right shape.
      Only its implementation changes (see (d)).

  (c) The plot method: base / derivative / antiderivative panels, y-labels as
      expressions. Ported to S7 essentially as is.

Four things get fixed in the port:

  (d) orthonorm via the exact Gram matrix, not via QR on a grid. The prototype
      evaluates the basis on npoints grid points, takes qr.R, and corrects by
      sqrt(npoints/(upper-lower)) -- that scale factor is the tell that the
      grid is approximating what the Gram matrix gives exactly. With
      G = int B B' dx = L L' (Cholesky), the basis B L^{-T} is orthonormal in
      L2([lower, upper]) exactly, grid-free, with no npoints argument and no
      scale factor. Derivatives and integrals transform by the same L^{-T},
      since the map is linear.

  (e) The integral CONVENTION, a real defect found reviewing the formulas.
      basis_int must return the definite integral from `lower` to x, which is
      zero at x = lower -- this is what splines2 returns for B-splines. The
      prototype's Fourier d = -1 returns AN antiderivative instead: at
      x = lower the sine columns are -omega/(2*pi*j) and the intercept column
      is `lower`, while the cosine columns happen to be 0. Half the columns
      follow one convention and half the other, invisible in the prototype's
      tests because lower = 0 there. Fix: subtract the value at `lower`. The
      convention becomes a permanent check in check_basis() (section 5).

  (f) The Fourier constructor REFUSES an odd number of trigonometric columns
      instead of silently growing `dimension` by one with a warning. The
      constructor is the only place an inconsistency can be caught, and an
      error is worth more than a plausible number. (fda auto-adjusts; we
      don't.)

  (g) gpf's Riemann sum becomes exact integration. For a spline of degree k,
      the integrand of G_d is piecewise polynomial of degree 2(k-d), so
      Gauss-Legendre per knot interval is EXACT with ceiling((2(k-d)+1)/2)
      nodes. For Fourier, G_d is closed form by orthogonality of the
      trigonometric system (diagonal, up to the constant column). The
      quadrature version survives only as the numerical fallback for
      user-defined bases.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE OBJECT MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One abstract S7 class:

    basis
      properties:
        basis_name    character
        dimension     integer            number of columns, K
        lower, upper  numeric            the interval the basis lives on
        basis_params  list               whatever the subclass needs

Subclasses, one per family, named CamelCase like the distribution classes
(BsplineBasis, FourierBasis, PolyBasis, TransformedBasis, TensorBasis), with
lowercase constructors (bspline_basis(), fourier_basis(), poly_basis(),
orthonorm_basis(), constrain_basis(), dr_basis(), tensor_basis()). Note the Windows Rd trap does NOT bite here --
BsplineBasis.Rd and bspline_basis.Rd differ by the underscore, not only by
case -- but the classes get documented at X-class with @aliases from day one
anyway, and test-docs.R asks "which object in the namespace has no help
topic", both habits copied from optimizers7 (CLAUDE.md, Documentation build).

TransformedBasis is the one wrapper for every linear reparametrisation
B -> B T (section 3c): it holds the parent basis and the fixed matrix T, and
every method delegates to the parent and multiplies. Same composition rules
as the distributions7 wrappers: transforming a TransformedBasis multiplies
the two T's rather than nesting, and orthonormalising twice is a no-op.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE GENERICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Four core generics. Derivative order is an ARGUMENT, not a family of generics
as in linkfunctions7: there the orders stop at four and the double dispatch
was worth removing in hot paths; here the order is unbounded for analytic
bases and the design matrix call is not a per-observation hot path.

    basis_eval(basis, x)                  n x K design matrix
    basis_deriv(basis, x, order = 1)      order >= 1; order > degree gives the
                                          zero matrix, documented, not an error
    basis_int(basis, x)                   int_lower^x B(t) dt; ZERO at x = lower
    basis_gram(basis, order = 0)          G_d = int B^(d)(t) B^(d)(t)' dt, K x K

plus print(), plot() (three panels as in the prototype), and check_basis().

No magic order = -1: the antiderivative has its own generic, as in the
prototype's own basis_int, and the -1 encoding stays an implementation detail
inside the Fourier evaluator.

The weighted Gram -- the prototype's gpf, i.e. the Gram of the differential
operator L_w = sum_j w_j D^j -- comes with basis_gram(basis, w = c(...)) as an
alternative to `order` once penalties7 asks for it; the scalar-order form is
the special case w = e_{d+1} and ships in v1.

Column names: "<basis_name><j>" (bs1, bs2, ..., sin1, cos1, ...), stable across
eval/deriv/int, because modelterms7 will build coefficient names from them.

Evaluation OUTSIDE [lower, upper] is an error in v1, symmetric with
distributions7's open-interval validation. Extrapolation policies (linear
continuation for P-splines a la mgcv) are a model-layer concern; revisit when
modelterms7 needs prediction outside the training range. Recorded as an open
item, not silently chosen.

NA handling: NA in, NA row out, never dropped -- and mind the NA^0 = 1 trap
recorded in CLAUDE.md: any column that reduces to a constant must propagate
missingness explicitly.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3b. MULTIVARIATE BASES AND TENSOR PRODUCTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Decided 2026-08-03: multivariate construction belongs to basis7. The reference
for the efficient path is Ruegamer, "Scalable Higher-Order Tensor Product
Spline Models", AISTATS 2024 (source in ../articoli/, local copy only -- a
copyrighted paper does NOT go into any public repository).

3b.1 The classic tensor product, exact and explicit.

    tensor_basis(b1, b2, ...)  -->  TensorBasis

A wrapper over a list of D marginal bases. The domain becomes the box
[lower_1, upper_1] x ... x [lower_D, upper_D]; `dimension` is prod(K_j);
evaluation points are an n x D matrix. The generics extend by making the
derivative order a MULTI-INDEX:

    basis_eval(b, X)                       row-wise Kronecker of the marginal
                                           evaluations, n x prod(K_j)
    basis_deriv(b, X, order = c(d1..dD))   product of marginal derivatives
    basis_int(b, X)                        the mixed antiderivative from the
                                           lower corner; separability makes it
                                           the row-wise Kronecker of the
                                           marginal basis_int columns, and it
                                           is zero on the corner by the same
                                           convention as the univariate case
    basis_gram(b, order = c(d1..dD))       SEPARABLE: G = kron_j G_j^(d_j),
                                           exact whenever the marginals are

Column order follows base R kronecker() -- rightmost marginal fastest -- and
column names are the dot-joined marginal names ("bs2.sin1"), fixed once,
because modelterms7 will build coefficient names from them. check_basis()
extends with a separability check: the tensor Gram against the Kronecker of
independently computed marginal Grams.

The Kronecker-SUM penalty (lambda_1 P_1 (+) lambda_2 P_2, and Wood's
G-weighted variants) is deliberately NOT built here: which combination a term
is shrunk by is penalty semantics. basis7 supplies the marginal Grams;
penalties7 assembles them.

The explicit tensor is the right tool for D = 2 or 3 with moderate M, which
is where almost all applied smooths live. Its costs are the honest ones:
O(n prod K_j) to materialize, O(prod K_j) coefficients.

3b.2 The contracted evaluation -- the Ruegamer path.

The scaling problem of tensor products is not the basis, it is materializing
the Kronecker product and carrying prod(K_j) coefficients. The paper's
observation, transplanted to this layer: everything a fit needs can be
computed from the MARGINAL evaluations alone, at O(n sum K_j) memory, if the
evaluation is contracted against the coefficients instead of being formed and
then multiplied.

    basis_contract(b, X, coef)

with `coef` in one of two shapes:

  - a full array of dim c(K_1, ..., K_D): the contraction runs by mode
    products, cost O(n prod K_j) time but never forms the n x prod(K_j)
    matrix, so memory stays O(n sum K_j) plus the coefficient array;

  - a CP (rank-F) factor list, Gamma_j of dim K_j x F: the term value is
    sum_f prod_j (B_j(x_j)' Gamma_j[, f]), cost O(n F sum K_j) time AND
    memory. This is one D-variate term of the paper's additive factorization
    machine, exactly; the marginal matrices Phi_j = basis_eval(b_j, x_j) %*%
    Gamma_j are what modelterms7's factorized term will ask for.

What deliberately stays OUT of basis7, with its destination:

  - the latent factors Gamma and their estimation      -> modelterms7 (a
    factorized tensor term is a term: it owns parameters);
  - the FM identity and the ANOVA-kernel recursion
    Phi^(d) = (1/d) sum_t (-1)^(t+1) Phi^(d-t) sum_j phi_j^t, which sum one
    factorized term across SUBSETS of features          -> modelterms7 (that
    is formula structure, not basis mathematics);
  - the df <-> lambda map by Demmler-Reinsch that makes one smoothing level
    comparable across terms                             -> penalties7 (record
    this in its plan when drafted; the ingredients are the marginal B'B and
    P_j, both available here and there);
  - the optimiser                                       -> optimizers7, which
    already ships adam(); the paper fits with Adam and minibatch objectives
    are one-line closures there by design.

The pipeline of the paper therefore maps one-to-one onto the toolkit's
layers, which is worth noticing: it is the first external method whose whole
architecture decomposes into the four packages without remainder.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3c. LINEAR TRANSFORMS OF A BASIS: ORTHONORMALISATION, CONSTRAINTS,
    AND THE DEMMLER-REINSCH CONSTRUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Added 2026-08-03, after Bach & Klein, "Bayesian Effect Selection in Additive
Models with an Application to Time-to-Event Data" (source in ../articoli/,
local copy only). The paper's estimation layer -- NBPSS spike-and-slab priors,
MCMC, the Cox model -- is modelterms7/statmodels7 territory, but its Section 2
is basis mathematics, and it exposes a unification this plan had not made:

    Every useful reparametrisation of a basis is one operation,  B -> B T,
    with derivatives, integrals and Grams transforming by the same T.

The prototype's orthonorm wrapper, identifiability constraints, and the
Demmler-Reinsch basis are all instances. So the wrapper class generalises:

    TransformedBasis        parent basis + a fixed K x r matrix T
                            (r <= K: transforms may REDUCE dimension);
                            every generic delegates to the parent and
                            multiplies. Composition collapses: transforming a
                            TransformedBasis multiplies the two T's.

with three constructors in v1:

  (i)   orthonorm_basis(b)          T = L^{-T} from the L2 Gram (section 1d);
                                    square, dimension-preserving.

  (ii)  constrain_basis(b, C)       C a q x K constraint matrix, meaning the
                                    coefficients are restricted to C beta = 0;
                                    T = an orthonormal null-space basis of C
                                    (by SVD), r = K - rank(C). The MECHANICS
                                    of constraining live here; WHICH
                                    constraints a model term gets (sum-to-
                                    zero, orthogonality to the linear part)
                                    stays a modelterms7 decision. This
                                    refines decision 6b rather than reversing
                                    it: bases are complete BY DEFAULT, and
                                    the constraint tool is available for the
                                    layer that owns the policy.

  (iii) dr_basis(b, x, K)           Bach & Klein's Algorithm 1, generalised:
                                    given evaluation points x, a penalty
                                    matrix K, and constraint functions
                                    (default: constant and identity, i.e.
                                    C = t(cbind(1, x)) %*% basis_eval(b, x)),
                                    project onto ker(C), then solve the
                                    generalised eigenproblem for the pair
                                    (V0' (B'B/n) V0, V0' K V0) and set
                                    T = V0 A. The result: empirical Gram
                                    DIAGONAL, penalty a scalar multiple of
                                    the identity, and (1, x)' Z = 0 exactly.
                                    An option rescales so trace(Z'Z/n) = 1,
                                    which is what makes one prior scale
                                    comparable across terms.

Why (iii) earns its place here rather than upstream: the paper shows the
construction only factorises a 2 x (K) and a (K-2) x (K-2) matrix -- never
n x n -- works when B itself is RANK-DEFICIENT (which equidistant-knot
P-splines produce whenever the Schoenberg-Whitney interlacing fails), and
keeps T explicit, so prediction at new points is basis_eval(parent, xnew) T,
which is exactly what a TransformedBasis does by construction. Their Figure 3
result is the design argument in one line: the mixed-model reparametrisations
(Fahrmeir-Kneib-Lang, Eilers) estimate the OVERALL effect as well as DR does,
but bias the linear/nonlinear SPLIT, because they do not satisfy
(1, x)' Z = 0; the decomposition semantics need the orthogonality the DR
transform provides.

One prerequisite generalisation falls out of the same paper: the DR
construction diagonalises the Gram with respect to the EMPIRICAL measure of
the data, not Lebesgue on [lower, upper], and the paper's estimands are
projections in L2(P^X). So basis_gram grows an optional measure argument:

    basis_gram(b, order = 0)              Lebesgue on the domain (default)
    basis_gram(b, order = 0, at = x)      empirical: crossprod(B(x))/n
    basis_gram(b, order = 0, weight = w)  int B B' w(t) dt, w a density,
                                          by the quadrature fallback

Two independent routes for the same matrix, which is what a check_basis()
check needs anyway (section 5.5).

The penalty matrix K is a PLAIN-MATRIX INPUT to dr_basis, deliberately:
penalties7 will depend on basis7, so basis7 cannot ask penalties7 for a
difference matrix. A caller today builds diff(diag(K), differences = 2) in
one line or passes basis_gram(b, order = 2); penalties7 will later pass
whatever object it owns, reduced to its matrix.

What stays upstream, with its destination: the effect-decomposition estimands
and their consistency theory (model semantics), the NBPSS/SSGL selection
priors and their MCMC (statmodels7's Bayesian side, already in the §1 vision:
"the user chooses frequentist or Bayesian"), and the choice of which terms
get decomposed at all (modelterms7).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE FALLBACK BARGAIN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Identical to distributions7 and linkfunctions7: a user-defined basis
implements ONLY basis_eval, and everything else works immediately.

    basis_deriv   central finite differences, obeying the one-stencil rule
                  that check_link() enforces next door: find the highest order
                  m available analytically and apply ONE stencil of order
                  k - m to it, never a chain of first differences. Steps
                  shrink near lower/upper so no evaluation leaves the domain.
    basis_int     cumulative Gauss-Legendre from `lower`, panel per requested
                  point interval, so the x = lower convention holds by
                  construction.
    basis_gram    composite Gauss-Legendre on [lower, upper].

Every closed form registered later takes over silently through S7 dispatch.
The vignette ("defining-a-basis") walks through exactly this bargain with a
basis the package does not ship -- and writing it is a test of the design,
the lesson optimizers7 taught: if the vignette cannot be written with exported
tools only, the package promised extensibility without supplying the parts.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. check_basis() -- the validator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The checks, each of which already earned its place elsewhere in the toolkit:

  1. shapes: every generic returns n x K (or K x K), with the declared column
     names, for scalar and vector x.
  2. derivative order k against ONE numerical differentiation of the analytic
     order k-1 (never nested numDeriv; by order 3 a nested reference is
     noise).
  3. basis_int: differentiates back to basis_eval, and is exactly zero at
     x = lower. This check exists because of defect 1(e).
  4. partition of unity, where the subclass declares it (B-splines: rows of
     basis_eval sum to 1).
  5. basis_gram: symmetric, positive semidefinite, and within tolerance of an
     independent quadrature -- independent meaning: if the method IS
     quadrature, the reference uses a different rule, so the check is not the
     same arithmetic twice (the cdf-derivative test-independence lesson).
  6. OrthonormBasis: Gram = identity to machine precision.

Test grids sit INSIDE the domain, deliberately, because numDeriv's Richardson
stencil reaches ~8e-4*|x| and a grid point closer than that to a boundary gets
differenced through points outside the domain. A separate test-extremes.R goes
TO the boundaries -- evaluation at lower and upper exactly, knots at the
boundary, degree-0 splines -- because that is where the defects live: 711
passing interior tests hid both linkfunctions7 tail defects.

Validator honesty, both injections from day one, paired so one cannot be
satisfied by weakening the other:
  - a WRONG method (derivative scaled by 1.05) must fail;
  - a MISSING method must be reported as [numerical], never as a bare PASS --
    the check_link() lesson, where an unimplemented derivative raised, left
    NA, and the summary reduced over it with na.rm = TRUE.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5b. WHAT PHASE 2 CHANGED IN THE VALIDATOR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Learned 2026-08-03, building phase 2, and it belongs with the checks of
section 5 rather than in a commit message.

check_basis() compared an exact derivative against a central difference, and
at a spline's OWN KNOTS the third derivative jumps, so the stencil returns the
jump rather than the truncation error and the BASIS is blamed for a failure of
the REFERENCE. A cubic B-spline of dimension 5 failed at x equal to its single
interior knot, with the analytic value exactly 0 and the reference 7.3e-5.

The fix is the device distributions7 already uses for a non-differentiable
parameter, in its continuous form: compute each reference at a step AND at
half of it. For a smooth point the two differ by three quarters of the
truncation, so four times the gap bounds the reference's own error; at a knot
the gap is large and says so. That bound becomes the slack the comparison is
allowed, point by point -- nothing is discarded, and the factor of four is
what halving the step does to a second-order stencil rather than a number
chosen to make a test pass.

Two rules this confirms, both already in CLAUDE.md and both earned again here:

  - a tolerance chosen on one basis is a tolerance chosen on one basis. The
    guard is checked across 22 cases -- every family, degrees 0 to 3,
    dimensions 3 to 31, shifted intervals, and the three transforms -- because
    a single fixed tolerance passed bspline8 and failed bspline20 for reasons
    that had nothing to do with either being wrong;
  - a guard that stops false failures must be re-injected against, or it has
    merely gone blind. A five per cent error still fails at every one of the
    22, by four orders of magnitude.

A third thing, specific to this layer: a TransformedBasis registers all three
derived methods, but each delegates to the parent and multiplies, so
basis_is_numerical() reports the PARENT's status. Without that, a transform of
an evaluation-only basis would have had check_basis compare a finite
difference against a finite difference -- the exact trap the [numerical] tag
exists to prevent, reintroduced through the wrapper.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5c. WHAT PHASE 3 SETTLED, AND THE SHAPE OF ITS DEFECTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Built 2026-08-03. Three decisions were made in the writing rather than in the
plan, and are recorded here because they are now contracts:

  - A basis DECLARES its input dimension through the length of its endpoints,
    read by basis_nvar(). A univariate basis is the case of one variable, not
    a separate kind of object, so nothing about the existing families changed
    except that they answer one more question. Evaluation points become a
    matrix with one column per variable and the derivative order a
    multi-index.

  - A single non-zero `order` is REFUSED for a product. It could mean that
    order in every coordinate or that total order, and the two are different
    matrices; recycling one of them silently would be a choice made on the
    caller's behalf. Zero is the exception, meaning no derivative under either
    reading.

  - The numerical fallbacks differentiate ONE coordinate at a time and refuse
    a mixed partial, and refuse the multiple integral outright. A mixed
    stencil carries the product of two errors, and no basis in the package
    needs one: a tensor product computes its own exactly. Refused rather than
    approximated badly.

The two defects the tests found are the same SHAPE, and it is the shape to
watch for in every layer that flattens a multi-index into a column index: both
returned a perfectly finite wrong number.

  - An R array is stored with the FIRST index fastest; the columns of a
    Kronecker product run with the LAST factor fastest. Flattening a
    coefficient array without reversing the dimensions pairs every coefficient
    with the wrong basis function. The fix is one aperm(); the danger is that
    a self-consistent test (contracting against the same wrong flattening)
    reports agreement.
  - The column NAMES had the same inversion, built with outer(), whose
    transpose reads plausibly and labels every column but the first and the
    last incorrectly.

And one latent since 0.1.0, exposed by the multivariate work: check_basis
compared c(n, k) against dim(), which is always integer, so a caller writing
n = 21 rather than 21L failed a check about the basis for a reason about the
argument. Coerce anything that will be compared with dim().

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5d. WHAT PHASE 4 SETTLED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Written 2026-08-03 as chapter 5 of the book, five sections in
book/chapters/_05a..._05e, gated by assert_expansions_ok(),
assert_families_ok(), assert_gram_ok(), assert_transforms_ok() and
assert_tensor_ok() in book/R/basis-certificates.R.

Two things had to be decided in the writing.

  - NOTATION. The book already spends l on the log-likelihood, and the
    truncation section had renamed its endpoints L and U for exactly that
    reason. The package writes a basis interval [l, u]; the book therefore
    writes [a, b], with r and s for basis-function indices, q for the
    intermediate degree of the Cox-de Boor recurrence, tau for the shifted
    Legendre variable and delta_j for a stencil offset. The notation appendix
    gained a Bases table and three more collision notes (B is a curvature
    model in chapter 4 and a design matrix in chapter 5; m is a spline degree
    here and a truncated expectation there).

  - WHAT MAKES THE GATE INDEPENDENT. Every displayed formula is transcribed by
    hand and compared against the package: the Cox-de Boor recurrence and the
    B-spline derivative recurrence against splines2, the Fourier phase shift
    at orders 0 to 4 and at -1, the first five Legendre polynomials written
    out as explicit polynomials against the recurrence, the two closed-form
    Gram diagonals, the Khatri-Rao column ordering and the polyadic
    contraction. The structural claims -- orthonormality, the congruence, the
    three Demmler-Reinsch properties, separability of the tensor Gram -- have
    no formula to transcribe, so each is checked against an independent
    adaptive quadrature or an independent numerical derivative, never against
    the package's own route to the same number. Seventeen injections, all
    caught; the Demmler-Reinsch checks were also confirmed non-vacuous by
    asking them of an orthonormalised basis, which fails them by 0.08 and 132.

The chapter found one defect in the package, and the way it was found is the
point: the numerical derivative printed with its rows labelled "c" and "f"
next to a closed form that printed "[1,]" and "[2,]". vapply had named the
stencil offsets after the character vector recording which stencil each point
uses, and those names travelled through the evaluation points into the row
names of whatever the basis returned. Only a user-defined basis could reach
it. Fixed in 0.3.1 with a regression test.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6a. splines2 as the B-spline engine: YES for v1. It is on CRAN, maintained,
    and its derivatives and integrals are exact and already tested; the S7
    interface hides it completely, so a native implementation (base R
    splineDesign plus the de Boor identity for the integral) can replace it
    later without any API change. It is the toolkit's first external
    dependency beyond S7/Rcpp; rebuilding B-spline integrals by hand out of
    pride is how one introduces the defects splines2 already fixed.

6b. No `intercept` argument anywhere: bases are complete BY DEFAULT. One
    argument fewer in every constructor, one branch fewer in every method,
    and partition of unity stays checkable. Refined 2026-08-03 by section 3c:
    the MECHANICS of restricting a basis (constrain_basis, dr_basis) live in
    basis7 as TransformedBasis constructors, because they are linear algebra
    on the basis; the POLICY of which constraints a term receives stays in
    modelterms7.

6c. Naming. The toolkit convention is now simply "the name ends in 7, the 7
    being S7" -- the plural-noun reading was dropped on 2026-08-03 (Giovanni:
    basis7 is better than bases7). Portal, book chapter 1 and CLAUDE.md
    updated the same day.

6d. No Rcpp in v1. Basis evaluation is matrix algebra, splines2/splineDesign
    are already compiled, and the 2026-07-31 measurement stands: assembly is
    BLAS, compiled kernels pay only for irregular elementwise arithmetic.
    Measure before adding any.

6e. Fourier omega default = upper - lower (period equals the interval), as in
    the prototype. An explicit omega is accepted and validated positive.

6f. v1 bases: bspline_basis (splines2), fourier_basis (phase shift, integral
    convention fixed), poly_basis (LEGENDRE by recurrence, shifted to
    [lower, upper], not raw monomials -- raw powers are numerically the worst
    choice and orthogonality makes the Gram diagonal), orthonorm_basis() as
    the wrapper. v1.1, when modelterms7 fixes their shape: truncated-power
    and step bases for seg()/jump(). Radial/thin-plate: later.

6g. Multivariate bases live in basis7 (Giovanni, 2026-08-03): tensor_basis()
    as the explicit classic construction, plus basis_contract() so the
    Kronecker product need never be materialized -- full-array and CP
    (rank-F) coefficient shapes, after Ruegamer (AISTATS 2024). The latent
    factors, the across-features FM recursion, the df-calibrated smoothing
    and the optimiser stay in modelterms7 / penalties7 / optimizers7
    respectively; section 3b records the split.

8. PHASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 1 -- skeleton.  ** DONE 2026-08-03, basis7 0.1.0 **
    basis class, the four generics with numerical fallbacks, bspline_basis
    and fourier_basis with exact methods, print/plot, check_basis with paired
    injections, test-extremes.R, full infrastructure of section 7.
    Exit test: an eval-only user basis (say, Gaussian bumps) runs through all
    four generics -- the same bargain distributions7 offers a density-only
    distribution -- and check_basis reports its derivative orders as
    [numerical].

Phase 2 -- inner products and transforms.  ** DONE 2026-08-03, basis7 0.2.0 **
    basis_gram exact per class (Gauss per knot interval for splines, closed
    form for Fourier, diagonal for Legendre), quadrature fallback, the
    measure argument (Lebesgue / empirical / weighted); the TransformedBasis
    wrapper with its three constructors -- orthonorm_basis via Cholesky,
    constrain_basis via SVD null space, dr_basis via the generalised
    eigenproblem of section 3c; poly_basis; vignette "defining-a-basis".
    Exit tests: (i) orthonorm_basis(bspline_basis())'s Gram is the identity
    to machine precision, via the exact route and the quadrature route
    independently; (ii) for dr_basis on data x: crossprod(Z) diagonal, the
    transformed penalty T'KT a scalar multiple of the identity, and
    t(cbind(1, x)) %*% Z zero to machine precision -- and the same three
    facts on a deliberately RANK-DEFICIENT B-spline design, since surviving
    that case is the construction's selling point.

Phase 3 -- multivariate.  ** DONE 2026-08-03, basis7 0.3.0 **
    tensor_basis() over arbitrary marginals: multi-index basis_deriv, the
    corner-anchored basis_int, the separable basis_gram; then
    basis_contract() with full-array and CP coefficient shapes.
    Exit tests: (i) the tensor Gram equals the Kronecker of independently
    computed marginal Grams; (ii) basis_contract on a full array equals
    basis_eval %*% vec(coef) on a problem small enough to materialize, and
    never allocates the n x prod(K_j) matrix on one too large to (checked by
    memory profiling, not by trust); (iii) the CP contraction at rank
    F = prod(K_j) reproduces the full-array value.

Phase 4 -- the book chapter.  ** DONE 2026-08-03 **
    "The basis7 package", one .qmd pulling _-prefixed sections: what a basis
    expansion is; B-splines and their local support; the phase-shift Fourier
    derivatives; inner products and the Gram matrix; orthonormalisation as a
    congruence; tensor products, separability, and the contracted evaluation
    with its costs stated. Hidden gate assert_bases_ok() in
    book/R/basis-certificates.R: printed formulas transcribed independently
    and compared (the link-formulas.R discipline -- a gate that compares the
    package with itself proves nothing), injection-checked before first
    render. Portal card flips from "planned"; sync-stack-files.sh carries
    the new chapter.

Phase 5 -- handoff.  ** NEXT **
    penalties7 gets the marginal Grams, the Kronecker-sum assembly and the
    Demmler-Reinsch df <-> lambda map (section 3b); modelterms7 gets complete
    bases, basis_contract, and the TransformedBasis constructors whose
    POLICY it owns (which terms are constrained, which are DR-decomposed
    into linear + nonlinear); statmodels7's Bayesian side gets, eventually,
    the NBPSS/SSGL selection priors of Bach & Klein, for which dr_basis is
    the prerequisite (a diagonal Gram, an identity penalty and a common
    trace scale are what make one prior comparable across terms). None of
    these is designed here beyond the interfaces already fixed in sections
    3, 3b and 3c.

9. OPEN, DELIBERATELY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Extrapolation outside the domain (error in v1; the model layer will
    say whether it wants linear continuation, and for which bases).
  - The weighted-Gram interface basis_gram(basis, w) (ships when penalties7
    consumes it; the mathematics is section 1(g)).
  - Truncated-power and step bases for seg()/jump() (v1.1, shape decided by
    modelterms7).
  - Natural cubic splines (splines2::naturalSpline slots straight into the
    BsplineBasis pattern; add when a consumer asks).
  - A sparse basis_eval for B-splines (each row has degree + 1 nonzeros; the
    Matrix package makes the design n x K sparse, and the GLMM experiment of
    2026-08-03 already showed sparse cluster designs paying off). Decide
    when modelterms7 fixes its design-matrix type; do not pre-build both.
  - Whether basis_contract should also accept a Tucker-format coefficient
    (core tensor plus factor matrices) alongside full and CP. CP is what the
    factorized model needs and full is what small terms need; Tucker waits
    for a consumer.
