Written in English like every other artifact of the toolkit, so that anything
here can be lifted into package documentation or into the book without
translation.

Nothing in this file is code. It is the set of decisions to agree on before
any is written, plus the ones deliberately left open. It grew out of the
penalties7 design discussion of 2026-08-03: a multivariate gaussian prior
needs a parametrised covariance, statmodels7's random effects need the same
object, and distributions7 cannot host multivariate distributions until the
matrix-parameter layer exists somewhere. This is that layer, built standalone
first, which is the reason it can start now.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. WHAT IT IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Almost every R package that fits a model with a covariance matrix carries its
own parametrisation of it, written inside the routine that needs it. nlme has
the pdMat classes (pdSymm, pdDiag, pdCompSymm, ...), lme4 has its theta
vector, glmmTMB has its covariance structures -- three private rewritings of
the same mathematics, none exposing a derivative, none reusable from outside.
Yet a covariance structure is a fixed mathematical object: the map from an
unconstrained vector to a symmetric positive definite matrix, its inverse,
its first and second derivatives, and the two quantities every gaussian
likelihood asks of the matrix -- the log-determinant and the solves -- are
all determined once and for all. They can be written once, correctly, for
everyone to build on. That is what covstructs7 is.

What it is NOT, and why:

  - It is NOT a collection of links, although it does the link's job. A link
    in linkfunctions7 is a SCALAR bijection with a diagonal Jacobian, and the
    whole link-scale machinery of distributions7 (Faa di Bruno with partial
    Bell polynomials) rests on that diagonality. A map eta in R^d -> Sigma
    has a full Jacobian -- dSigma/deta_k is a list of matrices, the second
    order an object with four indices -- and the chain rule is the
    multivariate Faa di Bruno (Constantine & Savits 1996, already in the
    book's bibliography). Different contract, different class, different
    package. The two COMPOSE rather than compete: the diagonal blocks of a
    structured matrix are scalar maps, and those are linkfunctions7 links
    reused as they are (section 3).

  - It is NOT part of distributions7, because its consumers are not all
    distributions: statmodels7's random-effects covariances (the GLMM tau^2
    generalised to Sigma_u) and penalties7's correlated gaussian prior need
    the object without needing a multivariate density around it.

  - It is NOT the general theory of constrained parameter spaces. The general
    concept covers the simplex (a multinomial's probability vector: a VECTOR
    parameter with a joint constraint, whose softmax/ALR transform has a
    non-diagonal Jacobian and is therefore not a scalar link either),
    row-stochastic transition matrices, factor-loading matrices, Stiefel
    frames. v1 is deliberately covariance-adjacent -- SPD, correlation,
    scale-times-correlation, the economical structures, and the scaled and
    summed precisions of section 3(g,h) -- and the name says so. The rest is
    recorded in section 10, not forgotten and not promised.

The design pressure is the toolkit's usual one: exact derivatives, on the
unconstrained scale, for arbitrary user-defined structures.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE OBJECT MODEL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

One abstract S7 class:

    covstruct
      struct_name     character
      dim             integer, the side p of the matrix
      n_free          integer, the length d of the free vector eta
      free_names      character, one label per component of eta -- fixed at
                      construction because every consumer builds coefficient
                      tables from them, the same reason basis7 fixed its
                      column names
      rank            integer, the rank of the matrix the family produces.
                      Equal to dim for the definite families; strictly less
                      for a rank-deficient precision, where it is the
                      dimension of the space the quadratic form actually
                      penalises
      null_basis      dim x (dim - rank) orthonormal basis of the common null
                      space, or a zero-column matrix when the family is full
                      rank. Carried rather than recomputed, and carried as a
                      BASIS rather than as its dimension alone, for the reason
                      in the note below; it is also what a consumer means by
                      "the unpenalised part of the model"
      role            "covariance", "precision" or "either". A LABEL, not a
                      branch: no method reads it and no mathematics depends
                      on it (decision 7h stands). It exists because a family
                      name does not say which side it parametrises, and the
                      distinction is a modelling one -- see the note in
                      section 2 -- so print(), a model's output and an
                      assertion by a consumer all need somewhere to read it
                      from

and one subclass per parametrisation (section 3). A structure OWNS its
dimension: log_cholesky(dim = 3) is a different object from
log_cholesky(dim = 4), with d = p(p+1)/2 = 6 and 10 respectively. The
alternative -- a dimensionless recipe applied to whatever arrives -- would
make free_names and n_free unanswerable at construction time, and both are
needed before any data exist.

Wrapper classes for composition (section 3d, 3f, 3h): a product structure
(D R D'), a block-diagonal structure and a sum hold their components and
delegate, the same pattern as TransformedBasis and TensorBasis in basis7.

RANK AND NULL SPACE ARE COMPUTED ONCE AT CONSTRUCTION, FROM THE COMPONENTS
AND NOT FROM AN ASSEMBLED MATRIX. The mathematics says they do not depend on
eta -- scaling by a positive number and summing PSD matrices both leave the
null space alone -- but the NUMERICAL determination of a rank from an
assembled matrix does depend on eta, badly. Measured on a tensor-product
penalty of true rank 28 out of 32, counting eigenvalues above a relative
tolerance of the assembled lambda_1 P_1 + lambda_2 P_2 gives 28 at
lambda_1 = lambda_2, 26 at a ratio of 1e8, and 16 at a ratio of 1e12: the
small contributions sink below the tolerance and are read as zeros. Over the
same range the null-space residual max|M N| / max|M| never moves off 3e-16,
and a fitted tensor product with smoothing parameters twelve orders of
magnitude apart is ordinary, not pathological.

So the rank comes from the STACKED, individually normalised components -- the
null space of a sum of PSD matrices is the intersection of the null spaces,
hence the null space of rbind(P_1/||P_1||, P_2/||P_2||, ...) -- and membership
is afterwards tested through null_basis, never by counting eigenvalues of
M(eta). This is the chol_pd lesson of basis7 one level up: there a
factorisation was mistaken for a rank test, here an eigenvalue count is, and
in both cases the fix is to ask a question about the matrix rather than about
the arithmetic that happened to be performed on it.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE GENERICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The naming follows linkfunctions7's direction convention: the MATRIX is the
constrained object (the "theta" side), the FREE VECTOR is the unconstrained
one (the "eta" side).

Core maps:

    struct_matrix(s, eta)         eta in R^d  ->  M, p x p, in the set
    struct_free(s, M)             M -> eta, the inverse map

Derivatives, exact to SECOND order in v1:

    struct_dmatrix(s, eta)        list of d matrices dM/deta_k, keyed by
                                  free_names
    struct_d2matrix(s, eta)       the d(d+1)/2 distinct second derivatives
                                  d2M/deta_k deta_l, keyed like hess_names --
                                  generated from the same enumeration as the
                                  names, never re-parsed from them (the
                                  deriv_indices lesson of distributions7)

Second order and not fourth, unlike the scalar links: the consumers actually
sketched -- joint Newton over (beta, eta), the Laplace/REML gradient through
the implicit function theorem -- need exactly two orders of M in eta. Nothing
currently planned needs a third. When statmodels7's higher-order asymptotics
ask for more, the contract extends; promising four orders of a matrix map
now would be exactness nobody consumes, at a real cost in derivations.

The likelihood byproducts, which are the point of owning the factor:

    struct_logdet(s, eta)         log|M| when rank == dim; the LOG PSEUDO-
                                  DETERMINANT, the sum of the logs of the
                                  non-zero eigenvalues, when rank < dim.
                                  Computed from the factor where one exists,
                                  never from det(M). The two are one generic
                                  because a consumer asks the same question --
                                  what normalising constant does this matrix
                                  contribute -- and the object knows which
                                  answer is the right one
    struct_dlogdet(s, eta)        its gradient in eta; in log-Cholesky
                                  log|Sigma| = 2 sum_i log L_ii is LINEAR in
                                  the log-diagonal components of eta, so this
                                  is sparse and exact by inspection
    struct_d2logdet(s, eta)       its Hessian in eta
    struct_solve(s, eta, B)       M^{-1} B through the factor, never through
                                  an explicit inverse
    struct_factor(s, eta)         the factor itself (L with M = L L'), for
                                  consumers that want to whiten

Identities the validator pins (section 6): d/deta_k log|M| =
tr(M^{-1} dM/deta_k), and d/deta_k M^{-1} = -M^{-1} (dM/deta_k) M^{-1}, so
logdet and solve derivatives are never independent claims -- they must agree
with the derivative matrices through these formulas, and check_covstruct()
compares the two routes. In the rank-deficient case the same identity holds
with the Moore-Penrose inverse in place of M^{-1}, restricted to the range
space, and the validator uses the eigendecomposition rather than a solve.

WHICH SIDE THE MATRIX PARAMETRISES CHANGES WHAT THE CONSUMER ASKS FOR, AND
THE PRECISION SIDE ASKS FOR LESS. For a multivariate gaussian written in the
precision, l = (1/2) log|Omega| - (1/2)(y-mu)' Omega (y-mu) + const, the
quadratic form is a multiplication and the score in eta is

    dl/deta_k = (1/2) d log|Omega|/deta_k - (1/2)(y-mu)' (dOmega/deta_k) (y-mu)

whose first term IS struct_dlogdet. Written in the covariance the same
quantities need Sigma^{-1} throughout, and the second derivatives need the
derivatives of the INVERSE, which follow from the identity above but which
every consumer would otherwise reassemble. So the precision route consumes
struct_matrix, struct_logdet, struct_dlogdet, struct_dmatrix and their second
orders, and needs neither struct_solve nor struct_factor; the covariance route
needs all of them. Nothing in the contract changes: the precision side is a
strict subset of it. The sign in front of the log-determinant is the
consumer's arithmetic, not the structure's -- the structure answers what
log|M| is, and the likelihood decides where it goes.

The label of section 1 exists because the FAMILY does not settle the question.
Some families are closed under inversion and some are not, measured: the
inverse of a compound-symmetry matrix is compound symmetry and the inverse of
a diagonal is diagonal (both to 1e-16), while the inverse of an AR(1) is
tridiagonal but NOT AR(1) -- it fails the defining ratio by 0.56, which is not
rounding. So "AR(1) covariance" and "AR(1) precision" are two different
models, and an object that cannot say which one it is produces a printout that
misleads. Unstructured SPD is closed as a family, but the map is not simple
either: the lower Cholesky factor of the inverse is not a rearrangement of the
factor of the matrix.

A rank-deficient structure answers struct_solve() and struct_factor() with a
refusal rather than with a pseudo-inverse. What a consumer of an improper
prior actually needs is the quadratic form and the log-pseudo-determinant --
the penalised normal equations invert X'X + lambda P, which is non-singular
even when P is not, and is assembled by the consumer -- so a pseudo-inverse
would be a plausible matrix answering a question nobody asked, which is the
shape of silent wrong answer this toolkit refuses.

Printing and validation round out the surface: print(s) states the family,
p, d and the free names; check_covstruct(s) is section 6.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE FAMILIES, v1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) log_cholesky(dim)  --  unstructured SPD. Pinheiro & Bates (1996),
    "Unconstrained parametrizations for variance-covariance matrices", is THE
    reference and settles the choice: unique, smooth both ways, no spurious
    boundary, and the log-determinant comes out linear in part of eta.
    Sigma = L L' with L lower triangular, eta = (log L_11, ..., log L_pp,
    then the below-diagonal entries of L in column-major order). The ordering
    is FIXED NOW, diagonal first, because free_names depend on it -- the same
    reason basis7 fixed the Kronecker column order before modelterms7 existed.
    free_names: "log_L11", ..., "L21", "L31", ... (exact scheme decided in
    phase 1, then frozen).

(b) corr_spherical(dim)  --  correlation matrices. The canonical partial
    correlations route (Rapisarda, Brigo & Mercurio 2007; the same object the
    LKJ prior is written against, which matters for statmodels7's Bayesian
    side): d = p(p-1)/2 unconstrained z_ij, partial correlations by tanh,
    the matrix assembled by the recursive formula. Unit diagonal by
    construction, positive definite by construction, every z free in R.

(c) diag_struct(dim, link = log_link())  --  diagonal positive. Elementwise
    scalar maps, which are linkfunctions7 objects reused as they are: the
    Jacobian of this block IS diagonal, the exact derivatives to fourth order
    already exist upstream, and this is the composition story stated in
    section 0. Also the degenerate but ubiquitous case
    scalar_struct(dim) = tau^2 I with d = 1, which is the GLMM variance and
    the ridge precision.

(d) dr_prod(d_struct, r_struct)  --  Sigma = D R D', Giovanni's own sketch
    and the reason the design is compositional: scales and correlations have
    different natures and different natural parametrisations, so the product
    wrapper combines a diagonal structure and a correlation structure,
    eta = (eta_D, eta_R), with the product rule supplying the derivatives
    from the components'.

(e) cs_struct(dim), ar1_struct(dim)  --  compound symmetry and AR(1), the
    economical structures: d collapses to 2 (a scale and a correlation-like
    parameter through tanh), which is the entire point -- a random effect
    over time with p = 20 occasions is 210 free parameters unstructured and
    2 here.

(f) block_diag(s1, s2, ...)  --  the composition the model layer actually
    hands an optimiser: several random-effects terms, each with its own
    structure, one block each. eta is the concatenation; derivatives are
    blockwise; free_names are prefixed per block. Flattens like
    tensor_basis() flattens, so the components of a composite are always the
    original structures.

(g) scaled_struct(P, link = log_link())  --  a FIXED matrix scaled by one
    free parameter, M(eta) = exp(eta) P. One free parameter, and the
    commonest penalty in semiparametric regression: P is a spline Gram
    G_d from basis7, a difference penalty Delta' Delta, a GMRF neighbourhood
    matrix, or the identity (which makes this the ridge). Everything is a
    constant times a power of lambda, so nothing needs deriving:

        dM/deta = M,   d2M/deta2 = M,
        log|M|_+ = rank * eta + log|P|_+,   d log|M|_+ / deta = rank,
        d2 log|M|_+ / deta2 = 0

    with log|P|_+ computed once at construction. The derivative of the log
    pseudo-determinant is the RANK, a constant, and that term is what makes
    lambda estimable at all: from -log prior = (lambda/2) beta' P beta -
    (rank/2) log lambda the stationary point is lambda = rank/(beta' P beta),
    whereas dropping the constant leaves a derivative of one sign and sends
    lambda to zero. This is the concrete form of the decision to KEEP the
    normalising constant. (A spline's smoothing parameter still wants REML
    rather than joint maximisation; the constant is what makes the quantity
    exist, not what makes joint maximisation correct.)

    P is required symmetric PSD and its rank is computed spectrally at
    construction. n_free = 0 is legal, giving a fully known matrix -- the
    same case as fixing every parameter of a distribution with fixed().

(h) sum_struct(s1, s2, ...)  --  M(eta) = sum_i M_i(eta_i), with eta the
    concatenation of the components' free vectors. The sum is linear, so the
    derivatives are the components' in their own blocks and the mixed second
    derivatives across blocks are exactly zero; nothing is approximated.

    A sum of scaled_struct components IS the multiple-smoothing-parameter
    penalty, lambda_1 P_1 + lambda_2 P_2 + ..., which is the anisotropic
    tensor product penalty of Wood (2006). piano_basis7.txt section 3b left
    that assembly to penalties7 on the grounds that "which combination a term
    is shrunk by is penalty semantics"; that is still true of the CHOICE, and
    the arithmetic of the combination belongs here. penalties7 decides which
    matrices go in; covstructs7 supplies the matrix, its rank and its
    derivatives.

    The rank of the sum is NOT the sum of the ranks, is not assumed, and is
    not read off the assembled matrix: for PSD components the null space of
    the sum is the intersection of the null spaces, so the rank and the null
    basis come from the stacked, individually normalised components, once at
    construction (section 1). Measured on a 32-column tensor-product penalty:
    the components have ranks 24 and 16, their sum has rank 28, and the sum of
    the ranks -- 40 -- is not even a possible answer. Two penalties each
    singular can sum to one of full rank, which is the normal case in a tensor
    product.

Factor-analytic low rank (Lambda Lambda' + Psi) is NOT in v1: its
identification constraints (which rotations are fixed, and how) are a
modelling decision, and building it before a consumer states them would
freeze the wrong ones. Section 10.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE FALLBACK BARGAIN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Same bargain as everywhere in the toolkit, adapted to the shape of the
object. Compulsory: struct_matrix(), plus the three declared integers/names
of section 1. Everything else has a numerical method registered on the base
class:

  - struct_dmatrix / struct_d2matrix by ONE finite-difference stencil per
    eta component (matrix-valued central differences; order 2 from a single
    stencil on the analytic order below when it exists -- basis7's
    numerical_deriv_matrix pattern, never nested same-variable differences);
  - struct_logdet and struct_solve through a Cholesky of struct_matrix(eta),
    with the PD verdict from the eigenvalues, not from whether chol()
    raises -- the chol_pd lesson of basis7 applies verbatim here, and this
    package lives on exactly that boundary. The verdict is taken AGAINST THE
    DECLARED RANK, not against full rank, and through null_basis rather than
    by counting eigenvalues of the assembled matrix (section 1). Falling back
    on "refuse anything singular" would reject the commonest penalty there is,
    and falling back on "count the eigenvalues" would reject a perfectly good
    one whose smoothing parameters happen to differ by several orders of
    magnitude, which is the normal state of a fitted tensor product;
  - struct_dlogdet / struct_d2logdet through the trace identities of
    section 2 applied to the (possibly numerical) derivative matrices;
  - struct_free by Cholesky-based inversion where the family's map makes it
    direct, and refused otherwise: a generic optimisation-based inverse would
    return a plausible eta for a matrix outside the set, which is a silent
    wrong answer of the kind the toolkit refuses.

So a user structure that supplies only eta -> M runs immediately, and
whatever it later supplies in closed form replaces the corresponding
fallback through dispatch. struct_is_numerical(s) reports which answers are
fallbacks, exactly like basis_is_numerical().


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT DISTRIBUTIONS7 WILL DO WITH IT, AND WHAT IT WILL NOT DO YET
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The eventual constructor is the second of Giovanni's sketches:

    mvgaussian_distrib(link_mu = identity_link(), struct_sigma = log_cholesky())
    mvgaussian_distrib(link_mu = identity_link(), struct_omega = log_cholesky())

-- a link for a scalar or vector parameter, a structure for a matrix one, the
argument's name says which contract the object must satisfy, and the two
matrix arguments are mutually exclusive: one names the covariance, the other
the precision, and the object records which through the structure's `role`.

The precision form is the one to prefer where the modelling allows it, and
the reason is arithmetic rather than taste (section 2): the density, the
score and the Hessian in eta are multiplications there and solves in the
covariance form, and the mixed block between the mean and the matrix
parameters is (dOmega/deta_k)(y-mu) against two solves.

One refusal belongs at that boundary and is recorded here so the future
mvgaussian does not rediscover it: a RANK-DEFICIENT precision is a legitimate
PENALTY -- an improper prior, the intrinsic GMRF, the whole of section 3(g) --
and NOT a legitimate DENSITY, since the normalising constant does not exist
and the law is supported on a subspace. covstructs7 accepts such a structure;
mvgaussian_distrib() must refuse one, and the rank the object already carries
is what lets it.

That constructor is NOT part of this plan. distributions7's theta contract
is "a list of scalars (or vectors along y)", and every piece of its
derivative bookkeeping -- align_theta, deriv_names, the Bartlett machinery,
the link scale -- is indexed by scalar parameter. Multivariate distributions
change that contract, and the change is distributions7's own future plan.
covstructs7 is built and validated standalone precisely so that the two
problems never block each other.

What CAN be consumed immediately, without touching distributions7:

  - penalties7's QUADRATIC branch, which is the first and largest of its
    three: the penalty beta' P(eta) beta with P a structure, its
    log-(pseudo-)determinant -- the normalising constant this session decided
    to KEEP -- and the derivatives in eta for estimating the penalty's own
    parameters. scaled_struct covers ridge and every single-smoothing-
    parameter spline penalty; sum_struct covers the anisotropic tensor
    product; log_cholesky covers an unstructured correlated prior. This makes
    covstructs7 a PREREQUISITE of penalties7 rather than an accessory to one
    of its branches, and it is the reason to build it before writing
    piano_penalties7.txt;
  - statmodels7's random effects, whenever that layer arrives: block_diag
    over terms, tau^2 I as the simplest block, exact derivatives for the
    Laplace machinery the GLMM experiment hand-wrote.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. check_covstruct() -- the validator
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The battery, all numerical, each check against a route the implementation
does not take:

  1. membership: struct_matrix(eta) is symmetric and PSD; a full-rank family
     is checked for min(eigenvalue) > 0 (eigenvalues, never a chol() success),
     and a rank-deficient one through its declared null space,
     max|M(eta) null_basis| / max|M(eta)| ~ 0, which is the test that survives
     an ill-scaled eta where an eigenvalue count does not (section 1). The
     sweep must include badly scaled eta -- components differing by twelve
     orders of magnitude -- precisely because that is where the naive check
     fails; correlation families have unit diagonal on top of that;
  2. round trip: struct_free(struct_matrix(eta)) recovers eta, over a random
     sweep of eta including large components (the tails file lesson: the
     defects live at the edges, tanh saturates, exp overflows);
  3. first derivatives against one central stencil on struct_matrix;
  4. second derivatives against one central stencil on the ANALYTIC first
     derivatives when they exist, and reported as not checked when both
     orders are fallbacks -- comparing FD with FD is the same arithmetic
     twice (the check_link / check_basis convention, including the
     [numerical] marking);
  5. logdet against the sum of the logs of the non-zero eigenvalues of
     struct_matrix (independent of the factor route, and the same expression
     in both the full-rank and the deficient case); dlogdet against
     tr(M^+ dM/deta_k) with the pseudo-inverse formed from the same
     eigendecomposition, independent of struct_solve. For scaled_struct this
     check has an exact target that shares nothing with the implementation:
     the derivative must be the rank, to the last digit;
  6. solve against base::solve;
  7. free_names/n_free/dim consistency with the shapes actually returned.

Tests pair every probe (the paired-injection rule): a structure made 5%
wrong in one derivative must fail, a structure with a MISSING method must be
reported as unchecked rather than passed, and the unbroken references must
keep passing next to both.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. DECISIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

7a. Name: covstructs7 (Giovanni, 2026-08-03), named for the v1 scope --
    covariance-adjacent spaces. The wider concept (simplex, Stiefel,
    stochastic matrices) is real and deliberately out; if it ever arrives it
    either widens this package's remit knowingly or gets its own home, and
    that decision is NOT being made today.

7b. Not links, by contract: vector-valued map, non-diagonal Jacobian,
    multivariate Faa di Bruno. Composes with linkfunctions7 (diagonal
    blocks are scalar links); therefore Imports: linkfunctions7 and nothing
    else of the toolkit. covstructs7 sits BESIDE distributions7 on top of
    linkfunctions7; penalties7 will import both.

7c. Exact derivatives to order 2 in v1, with the extension path stated in
    section 2. The scalar pieces that go through linkfunctions7 links keep
    their four orders for free.

7d. eta ordering and free_names frozen per family at phase 1, before any
    consumer exists -- the kronecker-order lesson from basis7.

7e. No Rcpp in v1: everything here is factor algebra and BLAS, which R calls
    at the same speed (the measured 2026-07-31 conclusion). Compiled kernels
    would buy nothing.

7f. The PD verdict is always spectral (min(ev) <= tol * max(ev)), never a
    chol() try/catch. Recorded as a package-wide rule, not a per-site fix.
    And for a rank-deficient family the verdict goes through null_basis, not
    through an eigenvalue count of the assembled matrix, which is not scale
    invariant (section 1, measured). Both halves of this rule say the same
    thing: a membership question is about the matrix, not about whichever
    arithmetic was performed on it.

7g. struct_free is exact-or-refused, never optimisation-based (section 4).

7h. A precision and a covariance are ONE class, distinguished by the declared
    rank, not two parallel hierarchies. The role is not a property of the
    family -- log_cholesky() is a perfectly good unstructured precision, and
    the same object serves either side -- it is a property of how the
    consumer uses the matrix. What genuinely differs is definiteness, and
    that is exactly what `rank` records: a covariance must be full rank
    (a singular one is degenerate), a precision may not be (an improper
    prior is legitimate and common). Two hierarchies would duplicate every
    family to express one integer.

7i. The scalar lambda of a scaled penalty belongs to the STRUCTURE, not to
    penalties7. It could go either way -- penalties7 could hold lambda and
    use a constant matrix -- but putting it here keeps the derivative
    bookkeeping in one place, and the one identity that matters
    (d log|M|_+ / d log lambda = rank) is a statement about the matrix.
    Reversible if a consumer argues otherwise.

7j. `role` is a label and never a branch. It is checked by consumers and
    printed; no method of this package reads it, and no mathematics depends
    on it. Making it a branch would split every family in two to express a
    fact about the caller, which is decision 7h again. The default is
    "either" for the families that are genuinely symmetric in use
    (log_cholesky, diag_struct), "precision" for scaled_struct and
    sum_struct, whose consumer is a penalty.

9. PHASES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Phase 1 -- skeleton.
    The covstruct class WITH ITS RANK CONTRACT, the generics of section 2,
    log_cholesky(), diag_struct()/scalar_struct(), scaled_struct(), the
    numerical fallbacks, and check_covstruct() with its paired injections.
    scaled_struct belongs in the first phase rather than a later one: it is
    the cheapest family to write and the one with a consumer waiting, and
    building it first is what forces the rank-deficient path to be real from
    the start instead of retrofitted. The eta orderings and free_names frozen
    here.

Phase 2 -- correlations and composition.
    corr_spherical(), dr_prod(), cs_struct(), ar1_struct(), block_diag(),
    sum_struct().
    The validator sweep extended to the composites, and the tails file
    (tanh saturation, log-diagonal overflow) written alongside.

Phase 3 -- the likelihood byproducts, hardened.
    logdet/solve/factor exactness at scale, boundary behaviour (near-singular
    M, large p), and the vignette: defining a structure from eta -> M alone,
    then adding closed forms one generic at a time -- the defining-a-basis
    template.

Phase 4 -- the book chapter, with gates, per the toolkit convention.

Phase 5 -- handoff.
    penalties7 consumes structures for its gaussian branch; statmodels7's
    random-effects layer consumes block_diag; distributions7's multivariate
    plan, when drafted, consumes the whole package and owns its own theta
    contract change. None of these is designed here beyond the constructor
    sketch of section 5.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. OPEN, DELIBERATELY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - The simplex and its relatives (softmax/ALR vector maps): the first
    non-covariance constrained space a multinomial will need. Whether it
    widens covstructs7 or starts its own package is decided when the
    consumer exists.
  - Factor-analytic low rank (Lambda Lambda' + Psi): waits for the consumer
    to state the identification constraints.
  - The matrix logarithm parametrisation (Sigma = expm(A), A symmetric):
    mathematically clean, computationally dear (every derivative needs the
    Frechet derivative of expm); add only if a consumer shows log-Cholesky
    failing it.
  - Toeplitz, banded, tapered structures: straightforward under the same
    contract, none has a consumer yet.
  - SPARSE STORAGE of a precision (Matrix package, GMRF neighbourhood
    matrices at large p). The singular precision itself is now v1 -- section
    3(g,h) -- but its representation is dense there. Sparsity is a storage
    decision that interacts with modelterms7's choice of design-matrix type,
    and basis7 left the same question open for the same reason: decide once,
    for both, when a consumer fixes it. Note the connection worth keeping:
    the log pseudo-determinant of a spline penalty is a sum over the non-zero
    Demmler-Reinsch eigenvalues, which basis7 already computes in dr_basis(),
    so the two packages hold the same quantity by two routes and each can
    check the other.
  - struct_invert(s): the structure of the inverse, where the family is
    closed under inversion in closed form (diagonal, compound symmetry, and
    unstructured SPD through a Cholesky of the inverse) and a refusal where
    it is not (AR(1), banded). Useful the moment a consumer parametrises one
    side and reports the other -- marginal variances from a fitted precision,
    say -- and speculative until then.

  - struct_dinverse / struct_d2inverse: the derivatives of M^{-1}, which the
    COVARIANCE parametrisation of a multivariate gaussian needs at second
    order and which follow mechanically from the identity in section 2. Not
    built now because the precision parametrisation avoids them entirely and
    is the one to prefer; build them when a consumer insists on the
    covariance side.

  - Third and fourth derivative orders of the matrix maps (section 2's
    extension path).
  - Rcpp, if a profile ever contradicts 7e.
