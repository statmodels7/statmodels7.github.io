Appendix A — Notation

Links

Symbol Meaning
\(\theta\) a parameter, on its natural (constrained) scale
\(\Theta\) the open interval \(\theta\) lives in, link_bounds
\(g\) the link function, \(\eta = g(\theta)\)
\(h\) its inverse, \(\theta = h(\eta)\), \(h = g^{-1}\)
\(\eta\) the linear predictor, unconstrained
\(g^{(k)}, h^{(k)}\) \(k\)-th derivatives, in \(\theta\) and \(\eta\) respectively
\(B_{m,j}\) the partial (incomplete) Bell polynomials

Distributions

Symbol Meaning
\(f(y;\theta)\) density or mass function
\(\ell(y) = \log f(y;\theta)\) log-density
\(\ell^{(i)}(y) = \partial\ell/\partial\theta_i\) score component
\(\ell^{(ij)}(y) = \partial^{2}\ell/\partial\theta_i\partial\theta_j\) observed second derivative
\(\mathcal{I}(\theta) = -\mathbb{E}[\ell^{(ij)}]\) Fisher information
\(\Pi_k\) the set partitions of a \(k\)-element index set
\(\mathbb{E}_f[\cdot]\) expectation under the parent distribution

Note the sign convention: the package returns \(\ell^{(ij)}\) and \(\mathbb{E}[\ell^{(ij)}]\), the derivatives. The informations are the negatives of these.

Transformations

Symbol Meaning
\(f_0 = f(0;\theta)\) the parent’s mass at zero
\(\zeta\) zero-inflation probability, parameter zi
\(\pi\) zero-adjustment probability, parameter za
\(L_0 = \zeta + (1-\zeta)f_0\) total mass at zero of a zero-inflated model
\(w_0 = (1-\zeta)f_0/L_0\) probability that an observed zero is a sampling zero
\(C_{ij}\) the hurdle’s truncation correction (Proposition 3.6)
\(g\), \(g^{-1}\), \(\lvert J\rvert\) a transformer, its inverse and the absolute Jacobian \(\lvert\mathrm{d}g^{-1}/\mathrm{d}y\rvert\)
\(\nu = \delta_0 + \lambda\) the dominating measure of a mixed distribution
\(L\), \(U\) the truncation points, both included in the support
\(Z(\theta)\) the retained mass of a truncated distribution
\(m_i,\ M_{ij}\) \(\mathbb{E}_T[\ell^{(i)}]\) and \(\mathbb{E}_T[\ell^{(ij)}+\ell^{(i)}\ell^{(j)}]\) under truncation

Optimization

Symbol Meaning
\(x\), \(d\), \(s\) the point, the direction, and the step \(s d\) taken along it
\(g = \nabla f(x)\) the gradient of the objective; see the note below
\(c_1\), \(c_2\) the Armijo and Wolfe constants, \(0 < c_1 < c_2 < 1\)
\(\theta_k\) the angle between \(d_k\) and \(-g_k\), in Theorem 4.1 only
\(B\), \(M\) a positive definite curvature model, and its role in \(d = -M^{-1}g\)
\(H\) an approximation to the inverse Hessian; \(\nabla^{2} f\) is written out
\(s\), \(y\) the secant pair, \(x_{+} - x\) and \(\nabla f(x_{+}) - \nabla f(x)\)
\(\rho = 1/(y^\top s)\) the BFGS scalar
\(\partial f(x)\) the subdifferential; \(v\) denotes one of its elements
\(v_j\), \(\alpha_j\) a collected subgradient and its linearization error
\(\bar v\), \(\bar\alpha\) their aggregates, \(V\lambda^{\star}\) and \(\alpha^\top\lambda^{\star}\)
\(\hat x\) the stability center of the bundle method
\(t\) the proximity weight, a step length on the parameter scale
\(\Delta_k\) the unit simplex in \(\mathbb{R}^{k}\)
\(L_j\), \(U_j\) the endpoints of the box constraining \(\theta_j\)
\(m_t\), \(v_t\) Adam’s first and second moment estimates

Bases

Symbol Meaning
\([a, b]\) the interval a basis is defined on
\(K\) the number of basis functions; \(B_1, \ldots, B_K\)
\(r\), \(s\) indices of basis functions
\(B(x)\), \(B\) the vector of values at one point, and the \(n \times K\) design matrix
\(\beta\) the coefficients of an expansion, \(f = B^\top\beta\)
\(d\) a derivative order; \((d_1, \ldots, d_D)\) a multi-index
\(G_d\), \(\widehat{G}_d\) the Gram matrix Equation 5.12 and its empirical counterpart Equation 5.15
\(m\), \(t_i\) the degree of a spline and its knots
\(q\) the intermediate degree in the recurrence Equation 5.6
\(\omega\), \(z\), \(j\) the Fourier period, phase and frequency
\(\tau\) the Legendre variable, \(\tau = 2(x-a)/(b-a) - 1\)
\(\delta_j\), \(w_j\), \(h\) a finite-difference offset, its weight and the step
\(T\) a linear transform of a basis, \(\widetilde{B}^\top = B^\top T\)
\(C\), \(V_0\) a linear constraint on the coefficients and a basis of its null space
\(P\), \(\lambda_k\) a penalty matrix and the Demmler-Reinsch eigenvalues
\(Z = BT\) the design matrix of a transformed basis
\(D\), \(K_j\), \(B_j\) the number of variables of a product basis, and the marginals
\(\ast\) the row-wise Kronecker product Equation 5.21
\(\Gamma_j\), \(\gamma_{j,f}\), \(F\) the factor matrices of a polyadic form, their columns and the rank

Matrix parameters

Symbol Meaning
\(p\) the side of the matrix, and the dimension of a multivariate response
\(d\) the number of free values of a parameter
\(\eta\) the free vector, \(\eta \in \mathbb{R}^{d}\)
\(M(\eta)\) the matrix a parameter produces, Equation 6.1
\(\mathcal{M}\) the constrained set the map is onto
\(A_k\), \(A_{kl}\) \(\partial M/\partial\eta_k\) and \(\partial^2 M/\partial\eta_k\partial\eta_l\)
\(L\), \(L_k\) the Cholesky factor and its derivative, Equation 6.4
\(\Sigma\), \(\Omega\) a covariance and the precision \(\Omega = \Sigma^{-1}\)
\(r = y - \mu\), \(w = \Sigma^{-1}r\) the residual and the weighted residual
\(q = r^\top \Sigma^{-1} r\) the squared Mahalanobis distance
\(c = (\nu+p)/(\nu+q)\) the weight of the multivariate \(t\), Equation 3.8
\(\varphi\) the concentration of a Dirichlet, with shapes \(\alpha = \varphi\mu\)
\(\pi\) the probabilities of a multinomial, written thus and not \(p\), which is the dimension
\(A = \partial\mu/\partial\eta\) for a simplex parameter, the Jacobian of the vector it produces rather than of a matrix; the two uses of \(A\) are told apart by whether the parameter is matrix- or simplex-valued
\(P\), \(\lambda\) a fixed penalty matrix and the scale it carries
\(\lvert M \rvert_{+}\) the pseudo-determinant Equation 6.9
\(N\) an orthonormal basis of the null space

Six symbols are reused across chapters, without ambiguity in context, and it is better to say so than to invent notation nobody uses.

\(g\) is the link function in Chapter 2 and the gradient here; \(h\) is the inverse link throughout, and in Section 4.4.1 it is exactly that, since the map removing a box constraint is a bounded link. The two meanings of \(g\) never appear in the same section: where Section 4.4.1 needs both, the gradient is written \(\nabla f\) in full.

\(\theta\) is a parameter everywhere except Theorem 4.1, where \(\theta_k\) is the angle appearing in the theorem’s classical statement.

\(\pi\) is the zero-adjustment probability of Section 3.2.3 and nothing else. The bundle method’s aggregate subgradient, which much of the literature calls \(p\) or \(\pi\), is written \(\bar v\) here. That choice is consistent with \(v_j\) for the subgradients it aggregates, and it avoids the collision.

\(B\) is a curvature model in Chapter 4 and a design matrix in Chapter 5. The two never appear together, and both are the letter their own literature uses. For the same reason the interval of a basis is written \([a, b]\) rather than with the \(\ell\) that would collide with the log-likelihood.

\(m\) is the degree of a spline in Chapter 5, while it is a truncated expectation in Section 3.2.5 and a moment estimate in Section 4.4.2. Each carries the index its own section gives it.

\(P\) is a penalty matrix in both Chapter 5 and Chapter 6, which is the same object, and \(\lambda\) is its scale in Chapter 6 while it is a Demmler-Reinsch eigenvalue in Section 5.4. \(\eta\) is a linear predictor in Chapter 2 and the free vector of a structure in Chapter 6; both are the unconstrained coordinate an optimizer works on, which is why the same letter is right for both.
