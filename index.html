statmodels7

An R statistical modelling stack built on the S7 object system, where every piece carries exact analytical derivatives.

The packages

linkfunctions7

Fourteen link functions with exact derivatives to fourth order in both directions, and a diagnostic that verifies them.

distributions7

Fourteen distributions with closed-form score, information and higher derivatives, maximum likelihood on the link scale, and numerical validation.

modelterms7planned

Terms that go into a model formula — smooths, segments, jumps, penalised bases — each defining its own design and derivatives.

basis7planned

Basis expansions: B-splines, P-splines, and the machinery to build new ones.

penalties7planned

Ridge, lasso, group lasso and the rest, as objects a fitting routine can interrogate.

statmodels7planned

The package that assembles the others into models: every parameter modelled, pluggable optimisers, frequentist or Bayesian.

What ties it together

Fitting a model means optimising a likelihood over constrained parameters — a variance that must stay positive, a probability that must stay in the unit interval. Every package in this stack takes the same approach to that problem: reparameterise through a link so the optimiser works on the whole real line, and supply the derivatives it needs exactly, in closed form, rather than by differencing.

That is why link functions and distributions come first. They are where the derivatives live.

A taste

# a distribution is an object, and it knows its own derivatives
d <- gamma_distrib()
y <- distrib_rng(d, 500, list(mu = 3, sigma2 = 2))

fit <- fit_distrib(d, y)   # maximised on the link scale
coef(fit)
plot(fit)                  # the fit against the data

# and a distribution of your own needs only its density:
# the cdf, quantile, sampler and every derivative follow

Conventions

  • Every package is a plural noun followed by 7 — the S7 object system, and the plural of what it provides.
  • Derivatives are exact and analytical wherever a closed form exists, and numerically validated where one does not.
  • Anything the packages compute for themselves, you can compute for a component of your own: the extension points are the same generics.
